Basic Setup

A mixed logit model is any model whose choice probabilities can be expressed in the form:

Pni=∫Lni(β)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.1)P_{ni}=\int L_{ni}(\beta)f(\beta)d\beta \tag{7.1.4.1}Pni​=∫Lni​(β)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.1)

where Lni(β)L_{ni}(\beta)Lni​(β) is the logit probability evaluated at parameters β\betaβ:

Lni(β)=eVni(β)∑j=1JeVnj(β)(7.1.4.2)L_{ni}(\beta)=\frac{e^{V_{ni}(\beta)}}{\sum_{j=1}^Je^{V_{nj}(\beta)}} \tag{7.1.4.2}Lni​(β)=∑j=1J​eVnj​(β)eVni​(β)​(7.1.4.2)

If utility is linear in β\betaβ (Vni(β)=β′xniV_{ni}(\beta)=\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}Vni​(β)=β′xni​), the mixed logit probability takes its usual form:

Pni=∫(eβ′xni∑j=1Jeβ′xni)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.3)P_{ni}=\int \left(\frac{e^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}}{\sum_{j=1}^Je^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}}\right)f(\beta)d\beta \tag{7.1.4.3}Pni​=∫(∑j=1J​eβ′xni​eβ′xni​​)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.3)

In most applications, f(β)f(\beta)f(β) is specified to be continuous. For example, the density of β\betaβ can be specified to be normal with mean bbb and covariance WWW. The choice probability under this density becomes:

Pni=∫(eβ′xni∑j=1Jeβ′xni)ϕ(β  ∣  b,W)dβ(7.1.4.4)P_{ni}=\int \left(\frac{e^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}}{\sum_{j=1}^Je^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}}\right)\phi(\beta\;|\;b,W) d\beta \tag{7.1.4.4}Pni​=∫(∑j=1J​eβ′xni​eβ′xni​​)ϕ(β∣b,W)dβ(7.1.4.4)

where ϕ(β  ∣  b,W)\phi(\beta\;|\;b,W)ϕ(β∣b,W) is the normal density with mean bbb and covariance WWW. The researcher estimates bbb and WWW. The lognormal, uniform, triangular, gamma, or any other distribution can be used.

Simulation

Now suppose the utility is:

Unj=βn′xnj+εnj(7.1.4.5)U_{nj}=\beta_n^{\prime}x_{nj}+\varepsilon_{nj} \tag{7.1.4.5}Unj​=βn′​xnj​+εnj​(7.1.4.5)

where the coefficients βn′\beta_n^{\prime}βn′​ are distributed with density f(β ∣ θ)f(\beta\,|\,\theta)f(β∣θ), where θ\thetaθ refers collectively to the parameters of this distribution (such as the mean and covariance of β\betaβ). The choice probabilities are:

Pni=∫Lni(β)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.6)P_{ni}=\int L_{ni}(\beta)f(\beta)d\beta \tag{7.1.4.6}Pni​=∫Lni​(β)f(β)dβ(7.1.4.6)

where:

Lni(β)=eβ′xni∑j=1Jeβ′xni(7.1.4.7)L_{ni}(\beta)=\frac{e^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}}{\sum_{j=1}^Je^{\beta^{\prime}x_{ni}}} \tag{7.1.4.7}Lni​(β)=∑j=1J​eβ′xni​eβ′xni​​(7.1.4.7)

The probabilities are approximated through simulation for any given value of θ\thetaθ: (1) Draw a value of β\betaβ from f(β ∣ θ)f(\beta\,|\,\theta)f(β∣θ), and label it βr\beta^rβr with the superscript r=1r = 1r=1 referring to the first draw; (2) Calculate the logit formula Lni(βr)L_{ni}(\beta^r)Lni​(βr) with this draw; (3) Repeat steps 1 and 2 for RRR times, and average the results. This average is the simulated probability:

P^ni=1R∑r=1RLni(βr)(7.1.4.8)\hat{P}_{ni}=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^RL_{ni}(\beta^r) \tag{7.1.4.8}P^ni​=R1​r=1∑R​Lni​(βr)(7.1.4.8)

The simulated probabilities are inserted into the log-likelihood function to give a simulated log likelihood:

SLL=∑n=1N∑j=1JdnjP^nj(7.1.4.9)\text{SLL}=\sum_{n=1}^N\sum_{j=1}^Jd_{nj}\hat{P}_{nj} \tag{7.1.4.9}SLL=n=1∑N​j=1∑J​dnj​P^nj​(7.1.4.9)

where dnj=1d_{nj}=1dnj​=1 if nnn chose jjj and zero otherwise. The maximum simulated likelihood estimator (MSLE) is the value of θ\thetaθ that maximizes SLL.
