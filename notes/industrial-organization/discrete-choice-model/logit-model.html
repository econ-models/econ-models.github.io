Limitations

The logit model has limitations in several aspects: (1) heterogeneous taste or preference; (2) substitution patterns; (3) panel data. Let's see the second one: substitution patterns.

For any two alternatives iii and kkk, the ratio of the logit probabilities is:

PniPnk=eVni∑keVnjeVnk∑keVnj=eVnieVnk=eVni−Vnk(7.1.1.30)\frac{P_{ni}}{P_{nk}}=\frac{\frac{e^{V_{ni}}}{\sum_ke^{V_{nj}}}}{\frac{e^{V_{nk}}}{\sum_ke^{V_{nj}}}}=\frac{e^{V_{ni}}}{e^{V_{nk}}}=e^{V_{ni}-V_{nk}} \tag{7.1.1.30}Pnk​Pni​​=∑k​eVnj​eVnk​​∑k​eVnj​eVni​​​=eVnk​eVni​​=eVni​−Vnk​(7.1.1.30)

This ratio does not depend on any alternatives other than iii and kkk. That is, the relative odds of choosing iii over kkk are the same no matter what other alternatives are available or what the attributes of the other alternatives are. The logit model exhibits this independence from irrelevant alternatives (IIA). Apparently, IIA assumption is not reasonable in the real world.

Nest, let's see the elasticity conclusion:

ηii=∂Vni∂pnipni(1−Pni)=βpni(1−Pni)<0(7.1.1.31)\eta_{ii}=\frac{\partial V_{ni}}{\partial p_{ni}}p_{ni}(1-P_{ni})=\beta p_{ni}(1-P_{ni})<0 \tag{7.1.1.31}ηii​=∂pni​∂Vni​​pni​(1−Pni​)=βpni​(1−Pni​)<0(7.1.1.31)

which means a smaller demand elasticity (ηii\eta_{ii}ηii​) is related to a lower price (pnip_{ni}pni​). In reality, we know that when demand elasticity is lower, consumers are not sensitive to price changes, so producers have larger market power and higher markup (p−MCMC\frac{p-MC}{MC}MCp−MC​), which implies a higher price. Therefore, the elasticity conclusion is not consistent with our observation in the real world.

The three limitations will be relaxed in different further models: (1) heterogeneous taste or preference: relaxed in Probit Models and Mixed Logit Models; (2) substitution patterns: relaxed in Probit Models, GEV Models, and Mixed Logit Models; (3) panel data: relaxed in Probit Models and Mixed Logit Models.
