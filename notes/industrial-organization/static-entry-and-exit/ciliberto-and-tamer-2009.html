Reference: Ciliberto and Tamer (2009)

Model Setting

The paper studies entry decisions in a complete-information static game with multiple firms. Each market is indexed by mmm, and each potential entrant i=1,…,Ki=1,\ldots,Ki=1,…,K chooses

yim∈{0,1},(7.4.6.1)y_{im}\in\{0,1\}, \tag{7.4.6.1}yim​∈{0,1},(7.4.6.1)

where yim=1y_{im}=1yim​=1 indicates that firm iii serves market mmm.

Firm iii enters if its profit is nonnegative:

yim=1[πim(y−i,m,Xm,εim;θ)≥0].(7.4.6.2)y_{im} = 1[ \pi_{im}(y_{-i,m},X_m,\varepsilon_{im};\theta) \geq 0 ]. \tag{7.4.6.2}yim​=1[πim​(y−i,m​,Xm​,εim​;θ)≥0].(7.4.6.2)

The game is one of complete information: the profit shocks εim\varepsilon_{im}εim​ are observed by all firms but not by the econometrician.

The central empirical problem is that the same realization of observables and unobservables may support multiple Nash equilibria. The paper therefore develops an estimation approach that does not require specifying how one equilibrium is selected.

πim=Sm′αi+Zim′βi+Wim′γi+∑j≠iδjiyjm+∑j≠iZjm′ϕjiyjm+εim.(7.4.6.3)\pi_{im} = S_m'\alpha_i + Z_{im}'\beta_i + W_{im}'\gamma_i + \sum_{j\neq i}\delta_j^i y_{jm} + \sum_{j\neq i}Z_{jm}'\phi_j^i y_{jm} + \varepsilon_{im}. \tag{7.4.6.3}πim​=Sm′​αi​+Zim′​βi​+Wim′​γi​+j=i∑​δji​yjm​+j=i∑​Zjm′​ϕji​yjm​+εim​.(7.4.6.3)

The first three components capture market and firm characteristics.

The terms

An important feature of the model is that these competitive effects are heterogeneous:

The model also allows competitive effects to depend on observable characteristics of the competitor. In the airline application, the main characteristic is airport presence.

Because equilibrium selection is left unrestricted, the model generally does not identify a unique parameter vector.

Instead, define the identified set

ΘI={θ∈Θ:H1(θ,X)≤P(X)≤H2(θ,X)for almost every X}.(7.4.6.29)\Theta_I = \left\{ \theta\in\Theta: H_1(\theta,X) \leq P(X) \leq H_2(\theta,X) \quad \text{for almost every }X \right\}. \tag{7.4.6.29}ΘI​={θ∈Θ:H1​(θ,X)≤P(X)≤H2​(θ,X)for almost every X}.(7.4.6.29)

Any parameter value inside ΘI\Theta_IΘI​ can generate choice probabilities consistent with the data under some admissible equilibrium-selection mechanism.

The source of partial identification is economic rather than statistical: the model itself generates a set of possible outcomes whenever multiple equilibria exist.

Exclusion Restrictions

Firm-specific excluded variables can provide additional identification.

Consider again the two-player game. Suppose that x1x_1x1​ enters firm 1's profit but not firm 2's profit.

If

x1→−∞,(7.4.6.32)x_1\rightarrow-\infty, \tag{7.4.6.32}x1​→−∞,(7.4.6.32)

firm 1 becomes unprofitable regardless of firm 2's action:

y1=0.(7.4.6.33)y_1=0. \tag{7.4.6.33}y1​=0.(7.4.6.33)

The simultaneous entry game then effectively collapses into a single-agent decision problem for firm 2.

The probability

Pr⁡(0,0∣X)(7.4.6.34)\Pr(0,0|X) \tag{7.4.6.34}Pr(0,0∣X)(7.4.6.34)

can consequently be used to identify firm 2's payoff parameters and the marginal distribution of its unobservable.

Similarly, extreme variation in a firm-2-specific variable can isolate firm 1.

Once the firms' own-profit parameters are identified, situations in which the competitor is forced to enter or stay out provide information about the strategic interaction terms δ1\delta_1δ1​ and δ2\delta_2δ2​.

Thus, the identification intuition is

excluded shifter→force one player’s action→eliminate simultaneity→identify payoff parameters.(7.4.6.35)\text{excluded shifter} \rightarrow \text{force one player's action} \rightarrow \text{eliminate simultaneity} \rightarrow \text{identify payoff parameters}. \tag{7.4.6.35}excluded shifter→force one player’s action→eliminate simultaneity→identify payoff parameters.(7.4.6.35)

In applications with limited support, exclusion restrictions need not deliver exact point identification, but they can substantially shrink the identified set.

Let P(X)P(X)P(X) denote the empirical conditional choice probabilities.

Airline Application

A market is defined as travel between two airports, regardless of direction or intermediate connections.

The main strategic players are American, Delta, United, Southwest, medium airlines, and small low-cost carriers.

The empirical model therefore allows the identity of the entrant to matter, rather than assuming that competition depends only on the total number of airlines.

An important measure of observable heterogeneity is airport presence.

For airline jjj, airport presence measures the fraction of markets served by the airline from the endpoints of the relevant route.

The competitive effect of airline jjj on airline iii can therefore be written schematically as

Competitive Effectij=δji+Zj′ϕji,(7.4.6.53)\text{Competitive Effect}_{ij} = \delta_j^i + Z_j'\phi_j^i, \tag{7.4.6.53}Competitive Effectij​=δji​+Zj′​ϕji​,(7.4.6.53)

where ZjZ_jZj​ includes airport presence.

The first component captures a fixed competitive effect.

The second component allows the competitive effect to vary with the competitor's observable characteristics.

Economic Interpretation

If

δji<0,(7.4.6.54)\delta_j^i<0, \tag{7.4.6.54}δji​<0,(7.4.6.54)

the presence of airline jjj lowers airline iii's profit.

If the coefficient on airport presence is also negative, then

∂πi∂(AirportPresencej×yj)<0,(7.4.6.55)\frac{\partial \pi_i} {\partial (AirportPresence_j\times y_j) } <0, \tag{7.4.6.55}∂(AirportPresencej​×yj​)∂πi​​<0,(7.4.6.55)

meaning that an airline with a stronger airport presence imposes a larger negative competitive effect on its rivals.

This allows a large network carrier and a small low-cost carrier to have different effects on competitors even when both simply appear as “one additional entrant” in the market.

Main Empirical Findings

The estimates provide evidence of substantial heterogeneity in airline payoff functions.

The competitive effects of large carriers such as American, Delta, and United differ from those of Southwest and smaller low-cost carriers.

The negative competitive effect of an airline also increases with its airport presence.

Thus, market structure cannot be summarized only by the number of active firms. The identity and characteristics of competitors matter for firms' entry incentives.

Policy Counterfactual

The estimated model is used to study the repeal of the Wright Amendment, which restricted service from Dallas Love Field.

For each candidate parameter vector consistent with the data, the authors change the policy environment and recompute equilibrium market structures.

Because the model allows multiple equilibria, the counterfactual prediction is naturally set-valued rather than based on a uniquely selected equilibrium.

The model predicts that repealing the Wright Amendment increases the number of markets served from Dallas Love Field.
