Model Set-up

The utility a consumer derives from a given product is a function of both a vector of individual characteristics, say ζ\zetaζ, and a vector of product characteristics, say (x,ξ,px,\xi,px,ξ,p). Here ppp represents the price of the product, and xxx and ξ\xiξ are observed and unobserved (by the econometrician) product attributes. That is, the utility derived by consumer iii from consuming product jjj is given by the scalar value:

U(ζi,pj,xj,ξj;θ)(7.2.2.1)U(\zeta_i,p_j,x_j,\xi_j;\theta) \tag{7.2.2.1}U(ζi​,pj​,xj​,ξj​;θ)(7.2.2.1)

where θ\thetaθ is a kkk-vector of parameters to be estimated.

Consumers with different ζ\zetaζ make different choices, and to derive the aggregate demand system we integrate out the choice function over the distribution of ζ\zetaζ in the population. Throughout we will take ζ\zetaζ to have a known distribution. This distribution may either be the empirical distribution of a characteristic, or a standardized distribution whose standardization parameters are estimated (unit normals for example, whose standardization parameters are a mean vector and covariance matrix). For notational simplicity, we will let θ\thetaθ include any parameters determining the distribution of consumer characteristics, as well as the parameters that describe the utility surface conditional on these characteristics.
