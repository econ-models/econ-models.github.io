Model Setting

To curb congestion and air pollution, Beijing allocates a fixed quota of new vehicle licences by non-transferable lotteries, while Shanghai sells them through auctions. An auction allocates licences to the households with the highest willingness to pay (WTP), but if high-WTP households also drive more (type-dependent externalities), a lottery may generate lower external costs. Which mechanism gives higher social welfare is therefore an empirical question, and it hinges on the heterogeneity of WTP for a licence.

The licence price in Beijing is not observed (licences are free and non-transferable). The key idea of the paper is that the WTP for a licence equals the consumer surplus from the most preferred vehicle, i.e. the extra cost a consumer is willing to bear to own a car. The paper therefore estimates a BLP-type random coefficients demand model of new vehicles in four cities: Beijing, Nanjing, Shanghai and Tianjin (market m=1,2,3,4m=1,2,3,4m=1,2,3,4), with year-month ttt from 2008 to 2012. Nanjing and Tianjin have no quota; Beijing introduces the lottery in 2011 (t>36t>36t>36); Shanghai runs the auction throughout.

Identification

(1) Unlike standard BLP, identification does not rely on the assumption that unobserved product attributes are mean independent of observed ones (BLP instruments), because product dummies absorb ξj\xi_jξj​. The price coefficient is identified because, in Shanghai, different households pay different effective prices (pjp_jpj​ vs. pj+bp_j+bpj​+b) for the same vehicle: the discontinuity in purchase costs between Shanghai and other cities, together with time variation in the average winning bid (23,370 to 69,346 Yuan), MSRP variation (36,000 to over 1.1 million Yuan) and sales tax cuts for small-engine cars in 2009–2010, regulates the magnitude of the price coefficient.

(2) Micro-moments identify heterogeneity: high-income groups account for disproportionately large shares of buyers, especially in Shanghai where purchase costs are higher, which pins down α1\alpha_1α1​ and the curvature of the WTP schedule. The random coefficient on price turns out small, so WTP heterogeneity is mostly driven by income.

(3) LmtL_{mt}Lmt​ (i.e. γ0,γ1\gamma_0,\gamma_1γ0​,γ1​) is identified by the gap between total new vehicle sales and the number of licences in Beijing and Shanghai, since replacement buyers do not need new licences; its variation over time and across the two cities pins down LmtL_{mt}Lmt​. The income coefficient in utility is separately identified from Nanjing and Tianjin, which have no quota. ρ\rhoρ is pinned down by the quota moments.

WTP and Welfare Analysis

The WTP for a licence by household iii is the expected consumer surplus from its most preferred vehicle:

E(CSi)=Eϵ[max⁡j=1,...,Juˉij+ϵijMUij]=1MUln⁡[∑j=1,...,Jexp⁡(uˉij)]if MUij is constant(7.2.3.22)\mathbb{E}(CS_i)=\mathbb{E}_\epsilon\left[\max_{j=1,...,J}\frac{\bar{u}_{ij}+\epsilon_{ij}}{MU_{ij}}\right]=\frac{1}{MU}\ln\left[\sum_{j=1,...,J}\exp(\bar{u}_{ij})\right]\quad\text{if }MU_{ij}\text{ is constant} \tag{7.2.3.22}E(CSi​)=Eϵ​[j=1,...,Jmax​MUij​uˉij​+ϵij​​]=MU1​ln​j=1,...,J∑​exp(uˉij​)​if MUij​ is constant(7.2.3.22)

where MUijMU_{ij}MUij​ is the marginal utility of money. Since utility is non-linear in price, MUijMU_{ij}MUij​ is not constant, so the closed-form log-sum formula does not apply; expected CS is instead simulated following Herriges and Kling (1999). Sorting households by WTP gives the WTP schedule (demand curve) for licences.

Given the WTP schedule and a quota QQQ:

(1) Under a uniform price auction, licences go to the QQQ households with the highest WTP, and the clearing price is the QQQ-th highest WTP. Total surplus is the area under the WTP schedule to the left of the quota.

(2) Under a lottery, licences go to random households among those with positive WTP, so the expected surplus is QQQ times the average WTP of all potential buyers.

Social welfare equals consumer surplus from licences minus the external costs (congestion, air pollution, carbon) of the vehicles driven by licence holders. External costs rise with WTP because high-WTP (high-income) households drive more and buy larger cars, which is the advantage of the lottery.

Main Findings

In Beijing 2012 (quota 259,800), WTP is very heterogeneous: among households with positive WTP, the average is about 46,020 Yuan (s.d. 60,041), and some households would pay over 200,000 Yuan. A uniform price auction would clear at 75,890 Yuan and yield 37.5 billion Yuan of consumer surplus, while the lottery realizes only 11.5% of it (4.31 billion), a loss of nearly 33.2 billion Yuan. The lottery does reduce externalities, but this is dominated by the allocative cost: net social welfare is −25.66-25.66−25.66 billion under the lottery versus 4.62 billion under the auction, a difference of about 30 billion Yuan in 2012 alone. An auction would also have raised 19.7 billion Yuan for the Beijing government, more than the city's 17 billion subsidy to public transit. Comparing WTP with external costs also implies that the welfare-maximizing quota is well below the observed one (111,404 with a 15-year horizon for external costs).
