Model Setting

The paper studies how Japanese Clean Energy Vehicle subsidies (2013–2024) spill over to Uganda, where around 90% of first-time registered passenger vehicles are used imports from Japan. Subsidies in Japan accelerate the replacement of domestic cars, push more used cars into the export market, and lower their landed prices in Uganda. To quantify the equilibrium effects on fleet size, emissions and welfare, the paper adapts the dynamic equilibrium model of vehicle supply and demand in Jacobsen et al. (2023) to an open economy, where inflows into the domestic fleet occur through first-time registrations (imports).

Time is discrete and indexed by year ttt. Each product jjj is a make, model and exact age a(j)a(j)a(j). The set of products available in year ttt, Jt\mathcal{J}_tJt​, contains products with positive inherited stock and products that can be imported under the import-age limit. The importable subset is It⊆Jt\mathcal{I}_t\subseteq \mathcal{J}_tIt​⊆Jt​. The model has three blocks: (1) static nested logit demand for vehicle services with an income-dependent discount factor; (2) endogenous scrappage of the inherited stock; (3) perfectly elastic foreign supply at exogenous landed prices, which together with scrappage determines the evolution of the domestic fleet.

Results: Japanese subsidies increase total emissions in Uganda, because fleet expansion outweighs the lower average emissions per kilometer of younger imports; but consumer surplus gains raise overall welfare.
