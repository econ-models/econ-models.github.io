The model has several implications:

(1) Higher R&D efficiency (λ↑\lambda\uparrowλ↑), larger innovation size (γ↑\gamma\uparrowγ↑), a larger population/market size (N↑N\uparrowN↑), and a lower interest rate (r↓r\downarrowr↓) all promote economic growth.

(2) The role of market power: if α\alphaα is very large (approaching perfect competition), monopoly rents are reduced, weakening incentives for R&D, and growth may stagnate. A certain degree of monopoly power is therefore a necessary condition for growth.

(3) The negative externality of creative destruction: the denominator on the right-hand side, r+λn^r + \lambda \hat{n}r+λn^, indicates that current R&D (n^\hat{n}n^) increases the future rate of destruction, thereby reducing the expected returns to future innovators. Business-stealing effect: when making decisions, private innovators do not take into account that their innovations destroy the monopoly rents of previous innovators. This can lead private R&D investment to exceed the socially optimal level (over-investment in R&D).
