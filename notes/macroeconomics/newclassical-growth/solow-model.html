Model Set-up

The production function takes the form:

Y(t)=F(K(t),A(t)L(t))(4.1.1.1)Y(t)=F(K(t),A(t)L(t)) \tag{4.1.1.1}Y(t)=F(K(t),A(t)L(t))(4.1.1.1)

Notice also that AAA and LLL enter multiplicatively. ALALAL is referred to as effective labor, and technological progress that enters in this fashion is known as labor-augmenting or Harrod-neutral.

The model's critical assumption concerning the production function is that it has constant returns to scale in its two arguments, capital and effective labor. That is, multiplying both arguments by any nonnegative constant ccc causes output to change by the same factor:

F(cK,cAL)=cF(K,AL)(4.1.1.2)F(cK,cAL)=cF(K,AL) \tag{4.1.1.2}F(cK,cAL)=cF(K,AL)(4.1.1.2)

The assumption of constant returns can be thought of as a combination of two separate assumptions. The first is that the economy is big enough that the gains from specialization have been exhausted. The second assumption is that inputs other than capital, labor, and the effectiveness of labor are relatively unimportant.

The assumption of constant returns allows us to work with the production function in intensive form. Setting c=1ALc=\frac{1}{AL}c=AL1​ yields:
