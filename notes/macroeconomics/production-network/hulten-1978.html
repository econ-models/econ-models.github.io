Hulten's theorem considers a multi-sector economy, where one sector can use other sectors' products as intermediate inputs. It shows that, denoting the total factor productivity (TFP) of sector iii as AiA_{i}Ai​, the % change of the entire economy's TFP can be approximated in the first-order approximation as:

dTFPTFP=∑i=1NδidAiAi(4.11.1.1)\frac{dTFP}{TFP}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}\frac{dA_{i}}{A_{i}} \tag{4.11.1.1}TFPdTFP​=i=1∑N​δi​Ai​dAi​​(4.11.1.1)

where

δi=piyi∑i=1Npici\delta_{i}=\frac{p_{i}y_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}c_{i}}δi​=∑i=1N​pi​ci​pi​yi​​

is called the Domar weight. Here, pip_{i}pi​ is the price of good iii, yiy_{i}yi​ is the production of good iii, and cic_{i}ci​ is consumption (and investment, if there is capital stock) of good iii. Note that, because the denominator and the numerator do not necessarily match when the products are used as intermediate inputs, the Domar weight may not sum up to one.
