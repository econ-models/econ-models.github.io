Market Imperfections and Policy Interventions

Market imperfections are modeled as reduced-form "wedges" χ\chiχ and have two properties: (1) market imperfections raise input prices: for every dollar of good jjj that producer iii buys, he must make an additional payment that is χij\chi_{ij}χij​ fraction of the transaction value; (2) these payments represent "quasi-rents," meaning they are competed away and can be seen as deadweight losses that leave the economy in terms of the consumption good

Importantly, market imperfections do not represent government interventions, which are separately modeled as production subsidies represented by τ\tauτ. The goal is to analyze how policy interventions τ\tauτ affect aggregate efficiency, taking market imperfections χ\chiχ as given.

Suppose market imperfections arise because seller jjj requires each buyer iii to pay a fraction δij\delta_{ij}δij​ of transaction value up front. Buyers can achieve this by borrowing working capital, which carries an interest rate λ\lambdaλ. Effectively, for every dollar producer iii spends on input jjj, he must pay χij=λδij\chi_{ij}=\lambda\delta_{ij}χij​=λδij​ dollars to a lender. These interest payments raise producer iii's production costs.
