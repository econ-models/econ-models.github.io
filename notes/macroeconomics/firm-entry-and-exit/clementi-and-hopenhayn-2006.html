Reference: Clementi and Hopenhayn (2006)

The model is a principal-agent problem, primarily focusing on the optimal contract design in the context of corporate financing and information asymmetry. It describes how an entrepreneur obtains financing from a lender to make an investment and how to design a contract that optimizes the payoffs for both parties when the investment returns are uncertain.

Model Setup

Now suppose there is an entrepreneur who needs some capital (working capital kkk) to invest. The investment full depreciates and cannot be recovered. The successful possibility of investment projects θ∈{H,L}\theta\in\{H,L\}θ∈{H,L} is highly random, with two possible outcomes HHH (high probability of success) and LLL (low probability of success).

The probability of success θ=H\theta=Hθ=H is ppp, and it will bring benefits R(k)R(k)R(k). Assume: (1) R(0)=0R(0)=0R(0)=0, which means no investment, no returns; (2) R(k)R(k)R(k) is increasing, concave, and smooth. The probability of failure θ=L\theta=Lθ=L is 1−p1-p1−p. If the project fails, the entrepreneur does not get any returns. The investment capital is from the lender. We further assume that both entrepreneur and lender are risk-neutral. We also assume that they can fully commit to contract. The discount factor is δ\deltaδ.

For the entrepreneur, we have the assumption of limited liability. It means the entrepreneur can choose to default on repayment, meaning they cannot make negative repayment amounts (i.e., they cannot incur losses beyond their own capacity). The information structure may be symmetric (the lender can also observe whether the project succeeds or fails) or asymmetric (only the entrepreneur knows whether the project is successful).

One-shot and Symmetric Information
