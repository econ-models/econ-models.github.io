In some more cases, the production function may be the combination of different production technologies.

For example, in Hsieh and Klenow (2009), the production technology is the combination of CES production function and Cobb-Douglas production function. First, the aggregate economy uses different advanced intermediate inputs to produce the final output with a Cobb-Douglas production function. Then, all types of advanced intermediate inputs are produced using CES production functions, with primary intermediate inputs. Finally, these primary intermediate inputs are produced with capital and labor as inputs and Cobb-Douglas production function as technology. Then capital and labor markets clear at the same time.

Some cases are more complex, with more combination layers, like Acemoglu (2002). First, the final product is produced with two different intermediate inputs (materials and capital goods) and a CES production function. Then, the materials are produced with many types of special machines and labor inputs, and the capital goods are produced with many types of special machines and capital inputs. Finally, machines are provided by machine providers with a fixed margin cost and a markup.
