Basic Model

To bridge the micro and macro economics, we hope that the total demand of the economy can be respresented by a so-called representative consumer, which is very difficult in practice. Traditionally, only homothetic preferences can satisfy such a requirement. However, homothetic preferences imply that the income elasticity is constant at 1, which is impossible in reality. Therefore, Muellbauer (1976) proposed Price-Independent Generalized Linear (PIGL) preference to solve such a paradox. The preference has two important two properties: (1) non-linear income elasticity (consistent with reality); (2) aggregation simplicity (easy in application).

PIGL preferences are often defined by expenditure functions, rather than utility functions. Expenditure functions indicate the minumum expenditures given the price level ppp and utility level uuu. PIGL expenditure functions are like:

e(u,p)α=(1−u)a(p)α+ub(p)α(2.2.4.1)e(u,p)^{\alpha}=(1-u)a(p)^{\alpha}+ub(p)^{\alpha} \tag{2.2.4.1}e(u,p)α=(1−u)a(p)α+ub(p)α(2.2.4.1)
