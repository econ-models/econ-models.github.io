Model

The original paper is titled "Who Marris Whom", and it's one of the most important papers to explain the matching phenomenon in the marriage market. It has many extended models.

There are III types of men and JJJ types of women. For a type iii man to marry a type jjj woman, he must transfer amount of income τij\tau_{ij}τij​ to her. There are sub-marriage markets for every combination of I×JI\times JI×J types of men and women. The marriage market clears when, given equilibrium transfers τij\tau_{ij}τij​, the demand by type iii men for type jjj spouses is equal to the supply of type jjj women for type iii men for all i,ji,ji,j.

Let the utility of type iii man ggg who marries a type jjj woman be:

Vijg=α~ij−τij+εijg(5.4.1.1)V_{ijg}=\tilde{\alpha}_{ij}-\tau_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ijg} \tag{5.4.1.1}Vijg​=α~ij​−τij​+εijg​(5.4.1.1)

where α~ij\tilde{\alpha}_{ij}α~ij​ is the systematic gross return to a type iii man married to a type jjj woman; τij\tau_{ij}τij​ is the equilibrium transfer made by a type iii man to a type jjj spouse; and εijg\varepsilon_{ijg}εijg​ is an independently and identically distributed random variable with a type-I EV distribution. Therefore, the payoff to a man ggg from marrying a type jjj woman consists of two components: a systematic and an idiosyncratic component. The systematic component, α~ij−τij\tilde{\alpha}_{ij}-\tau_{ij}α~ij​−τij​, is common to all type iii men married to type jjj women. This systematic return is reduced when τij\tau_{ij}τij​, the equilibrium transfer, is increased.

The idiosyncratic component, εijg\varepsilon_{ijg}εijg​, measures the departure of ggg's individual-specific match payoff, VijgV_{ijg}Vijg​, from the systematic component. We assume that the distribution of εijg\varepsilon_{ijg}εijg​ does not depend on the specific type jjj woman that he may marry. The payoff to ggg from remaining unmarried, denoted by j=0j=0j=0, is:

Vi0g=α~i0+εi0g(5.4.1.2)V_{i0g}=\tilde{\alpha}_{i0}+\varepsilon_{i0g} \tag{5.4.1.2}Vi0g​=α~i0​+εi0g​(5.4.1.2)

where εi0g\varepsilon_{i0g}εi0g​ is also an IID random variable with a type-I EV distribution.

Individual ggg will choose according to:

Vig=max⁡j  {Vi0g,Vi1g,...,ViJg}(5.4.1.3)V_{ig}=\mathop{\max}_j \; \{V_{i0g},V_{i1g},...,V_{iJg}\} \tag{5.4.1.3}Vig​=maxj​{Vi0g​,Vi1g​,...,ViJg​}(5.4.1.3)

Observing Πij\Pi_{ij}Πij​ is not sufficient for us to identify the individual-specific systematic returns, αij\alpha_{ij}αij​ and γij\gamma_{ij}γij​. However, we have conditions:

and
