Model Setting

Generation projects that want to connect to the PJM grid go through an interconnection queue: a sequence of up to three engineering studies, each of which updates the project's interconnection cost. Most projects that enter do not complete. A long queue delays the studies (congestion externality), while the reduced-form evidence finds no quantitatively important cost or local output externalities. The paper builds a dynamic equilibrium model in which entry, waiting, withdrawal, completion and queue congestion are jointly determined, and uses it to evaluate study speedups and entry fees/deposits.

Time is discrete, t=1,2,…t=1,2,\dotst=1,2,… in quarters. Each project solves a finite-horizon optimal stopping problem. A potential project arrives at the start of a period and decides whether to pay an entry cost and join the queue. Projects that join receive up to three studies; between studies they pay waiting costs and can withdraw at any time, and each period they also face an exogenous withdrawal probability (failed permitting, expired leases, etc.). The last required study is the terminal study, after which the project decides whether to complete or withdraw. The first or second study can be terminal; the third always is. After a non-terminal first study the project is in Stage 1 (waiting for the second study); after a non-terminal second study it is in Stage 2 (waiting for the third). A project waits at most T=32T=32T=32 quarters; one that reaches TTT receives its terminal study immediately.

Projects do not interact directly. Each project responds to a vector of aggregate variables, the queue status, which determines its study arrival probabilities and its operating revenues. The queue status evolves deterministically because projects are atomistic.

Queue Status and Equilibrium

Each period an exogenous mass of potential projects Nt(x)N_t(x)Nt​(x) arrives. The entering mass then shrinks deterministically through endogenous withdrawals, exogenous withdrawals and completions. Study arrival probabilities and revenues depend on the queue-status vector Kt(e,x)\mathcal{K}_t(e,x)Kt​(e,x), which has three components: (1) the number of queued projects with weakly higher priority (arrived in e~≤e\tilde{e}\le ee~≤e), i.e. the position in the PJM-wide queue; (2) the number of such higher-priority projects in the same TO; (3) the capacity of each fuel type in service at the start of ttt. (1)–(2) affect study arrival; (3) affects r~it\tilde{r}_{it}r~it​ through wholesale prices. Projects take the path {Kt(e,x)}t\{\mathcal{K}_t(e,x)\}_t{Kt​(e,x)}t​ as given.

Optimal strategies are cutoff rules: a project waits if accumulated ξ\xiξ exceeds a threshold that depends on observed study information and characteristics. Under conditions in the paper's appendix, endogenous withdrawals occur only immediately after a study arrives; in periods without a study, only exogenous withdrawals occur, with probability 1−δ(⋅)1-\delta^{(\cdot)}1−δ(⋅).

A queuing equilibrium is a path of queue statuses {Kt(e,x)}t\{\mathcal{K}_t(e,x)\}_t{Kt​(e,x)}t​, strategies, masses and revenues such that: (i) entry, waiting and completion strategies are optimal given beliefs; (ii) beliefs about study arrivals, revenues and the joint distribution of costs and study information are consistent with the queue statuses; (iii) the queue-status path is generated by the entry, withdrawal and completion decisions. It is a fixed point. This is a large-market rational-expectations equilibrium for a nonstationary environment, close to (nonstationary) oblivious equilibrium: each project conditions on its own state and the equilibrium path of aggregate states, rather than on all other projects' states as in a Markov-perfect equilibrium (Ericson and Pakes 1995).

Estimation

The estimation sample is projects entering 2012–2020 (2,709 projects), with actions through 2023Q1. Three steps.

(1) Transitions and exogenous withdrawal, estimated directly from the data and used as beliefs. Study arrival: a hazard model with a probit link in queue position (priority is based only on entry date, and PJM does not speed up or delay studies by profitability, so survivor selection does not bias arrival estimates).
