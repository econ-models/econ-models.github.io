Model Setting

The model studies the dynamic entry decisions of wind farm investors under uncertainty about the renewal of the Production Tax Credit (PTC). Time is discrete and indexed by year ttt. A wind farm iii first decides whether to enter the market or wait. Conditional on entry, it can either negotiate a long-term Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with a utility jjj, or sell to non-utility buyers such as corporations and merchant hedge counterparties.

The model therefore consists of three components: bilateral bargaining between wind farms and utilities, buyer choice and non-utility demand, and dynamic entry under policy uncertainty. The first two components determine the expected profit from entering in year ttt, while the dynamic problem determines the optimal timing of entry.

Dynamic Entry under Policy Uncertainty

Projects that have remained in the interconnection queue for at least two years are treated as potential entrants. In year ttt, potential entrant iii faces an entry cost:

ψit=κWit+νit,(8.2.3.11)\psi_{it} = \kappa W_{it} + \nu_{it}, \tag{8.2.3.11}ψit​=κWit​+νit​,(8.2.3.11)

Policy Beliefs

Since an unrestricted sequence of future beliefs cannot be identified from observed entry decisions, the model imposes two restrictions.

First, policy termination is an absorbing state:

Pr⁡(ωt+1=0∣ωt=0)=1.(8.2.3.16)\Pr(\omega_{t+1}=0\mid\omega_t=0)=1. \tag{8.2.3.16}Pr(ωt+1​=0∣ωt​=0)=1.(8.2.3.16)

Thus, if the PTC expires, investors believe that it will never return. The value function without the PTC is stationary:

V0(sit,νit)=max⁡{π0(sit)−κWit−νit,  βE[V0(sit+1,νit+1)∣sit]}.(8.2.3.17)V^0(s_{it},\nu_{it}) = \max \left\{ \pi^0(s_{it}) - \kappa W_{it} - \nu_{it}, \; \beta E[V^0(s_{it+1},\nu_{it+1})\mid s_{it}] \right\}. \tag{8.2.3.17}V0(sit​,νit​)=max{π0(sit​)−κWit​−νit​,βE[V0(sit+1​,νit+1​)∣sit​]}.(8.2.3.17)

Second, conditional on the policy remaining active, investors in year ttt apply the same perceived one-year renewal probability btb_tbt​ to all future years. Beliefs can nevertheless change across cohorts as new information arrives. The value function when the PTC is currently active becomes:

V1(sit,νit;bt)=max⁡{π1(sit)−κWit−νit,β[btE[V1(sit+1,νit+1;bt)∣sit]+(1−bt)E[V0(sit+1,νit+1)∣sit]]}.(8.2.3.18)\begin{aligned} V^1(s_{it},\nu_{it};b_t) = \max \Big\{ & \pi^1(s_{it}) - \kappa W_{it} - \nu_{it}, \\ & \beta \left[ b_tE[V^1(s_{it+1},\nu_{it+1};b_t)\mid s_{it}] +(1-b_t) E[V^0(s_{it+1},\nu_{it+1})\mid s_{it}] \right] \Big\}. \end{aligned} \tag{8.2.3.18}V1(sit​,νit​;bt​)=max{​π1(sit​)−κWit​−νit​,β[bt​E[V1(sit+1​,νit+1​;bt​)∣sit​]+(1−bt​)E[V0(sit+1​,νit+1​)∣sit​]]}.​(8.2.3.18)

These assumptions transform the original non-stationary problem into a sequence of cohort-specific stationary dynamic problems indexed by btb_tbt​.

State Variables and Equilibrium

The state vector contains three groups of variables. The first group consists of time-varying exogenous states, including average turbine productivity, turbine prices, subsidy levels, electricity prices, and renewable credit prices. Their transitions are modeled as AR(1) processes.

The second group contains time-invariant project characteristics that affect utility demand, non-utility demand, and matching costs.

The third group captures the endogenous evolution of utilities' renewable demand. Since tracking the renewable portfolio gap of every potential utility is computationally infeasible, the model summarizes buyer-side demand using the inclusive value:

Φit=πit({Qjtgap}j∈Jit)−πit({Qjtgap=0}j∈Jit).(8.2.3.19)\Phi_{it} = \pi_{it}(\{Q^{gap}_{jt}\}_{j\in J_{it}}) - \pi_{it}(\{Q^{gap}_{jt}=0\}_{j\in J_{it}}). \tag{8.2.3.19}Φit​=πit​({Qjtgap​}j∈Jit​​)−πit​({Qjtgap​=0}j∈Jit​​).(8.2.3.19)

More wind capacity entering today reduces utilities' future renewable portfolio gaps and therefore lowers future Φit\Phi_{it}Φit​. Its transition depends on lagged new wind capacity KmtK_{mt}Kmt​. The model uses a moment-based Markov equilibrium in which investors form expectations about these aggregate industry states and make entry decisions according to their perceived policy beliefs.

Identification and Estimation: Bargaining and Buyer Choice

The optimal capacity and pricing equations are jointly estimated by nonlinear least squares. The turbine-cost convexity parameter γ2\gamma_2γ2​ is identified by how negotiated capacity responds to the per-unit subsidy. The hassle-cost parameter δ\deltaδ is identified by the effect of utilities' renewable portfolio gaps on negotiated capacity.

The bargaining parameter ρ\rhoρ is identified from the relative pass-through of utilities' willingness to pay and turbine costs into negotiated PPA prices. A larger ρ\rhoρ implies that negotiated prices move more closely with wind farm costs and less closely with utility willingness to pay.

The non-utility demand equation is estimated using instrumental variables because pinup_i^{nu}pinu​ is correlated with unobserved demand shocks. The instruments include renewable credit prices, agricultural land prices, and state-level wind energy tax and recruitment policies.

The buyer choice model is estimated by maximum likelihood. Matching-cost parameters (γ3,γ4)(\gamma_3,\gamma_4)(γ3​,γ4​) are identified by how matching probabilities vary with state borders and geographic distance, while σ\sigmaσ is identified by residual variation in utility choices. The non-utility probabilities μm\mu_mμm​ are identified by observed frequencies of non-utility contracts across markets.

Identification and Estimation: Dynamic Entry

The main identification challenge is to separate the entry-cost parameters (κ,ϕ)(\kappa,\phi)(κ,ϕ) from the policy-belief parameters btb_tbt​. High observed entry can result either from low entry costs or from pessimistic beliefs about future PTC renewal.

The identification strategy exploits variation in the policy regime over time. The baseline estimation assumes no policy uncertainty during 2014–2018:

The relatively stable entry behavior in this period is used to identify the entry-cost distribution. The model uses 2013–2018 as an alternative specification.

Key Estimates

The estimated bargaining weight of utilities is approximately 0.670.670.67, implying that utilities capture about two thirds of the gains from trade. The estimated price elasticity of non-utility wind demand is approximately −1.6-1.6−1.6, and about 24%24\%24% of wind farms sell to non-utility buyers.

The estimated mean realized entry cost conditional on entry is approximately $35 million. Most importantly, the perceived PTC renewal probability for the 2011 cohort is only around 0.30.30.3, while it recovers to approximately 0.840.840.84 for the 2012 cohort. The low estimated belief in 2011 generates a low continuation value of waiting and accounts for the large investment spike around the policy expiration date.
