Model Setting

The paper studies how legal risk from the permitting process (environmental and land-use permit litigation) affects the entry of utility-scale wind and solar projects in the U.S. Litigation history raises perceived risk and induces regulatory agencies to review permits more cautiously, while legal precedent clarifies legal standards. Because precedent is non-rival and non-excludable, but the developer who pursues an appeal bears the full litigation cost, the model is used to (1) quantify permitting costs in dollars, (2) correct for selection into the observed litigation and appellate sample, and (3) quantify the under-investment in precedent.

Each county-year is a local development market ccc with a finite set of potential projects i∈{1,…,Ic}i\in\{1,\dots,I_c\}i∈{1,…,Ic​}. The game has two stages: in Stage 1, potential developers simultaneously decide whether to enter and start permitting; in Stage 2, entrants that are hit by lower-court litigation decide whether to engage in appellate proceedings (appeal, or defend a favorable ruling against an appeal). Information on market fundamentals is complete; each stage has an idiosyncratic private shock. Developers are short-lived: they anticipate litigation when entering, but take the legal and permitting environment as given and do not internalize how their actions change the environment of future cohorts.

At the start of each period all developers observe the common state:

Sc=(Lc,Dc,Mc,Cc)(8.2.4.1)S_c=(L_c,D_c,M_c,C_c) \tag{8.2.4.1}Sc​=(Lc​,Dc​,Mc​,Cc​)(8.2.4.1)

where LcL_cLc​ is the legal environment (number of past cases, cases with negative outcomes, precedent-setting rulings, lower- and appellate-court caseloads); DcD_cDc​ is the permitting environment (backlog of individual and general permits at the lead agency); McM_cMc​ is electricity market conditions (wholesale prices, PPA prices, demand); CcC_cCc​ is county characteristics (population, median income, Democratic vote share, queued higher-priority projects, zoning ordinances, grid congestion). Each project has observed characteristics xix_ixi​ (fuel type, capacity).

Legal Precedent as an Externality

One precedent shifts future lower-court litigation outcomes by δα>0\delta_\alpha>0δα​>0 and δβ>0\delta_\beta>0δβ​>0. Ω=[δα(1−pˉlit)+δβ pˉlit]κcomp ΠˉfutNfut(8.2.4.14)\Omega=\left[\delta_\alpha\left(1-\bar{p}^{lit}\right)+\delta_\beta\,\bar{p}^{lit}\right]\kappa^{comp}\,\bar{\Pi}^{fut}N^{fut} \tag{8.2.4.14}Ω=[δα​(1−pˉ​lit)+δβ​pˉ​lit]κcompΠˉfutNfut(8.2.4.14)

The external benefit of project iii's appellate engagement is Ωi=λiΩ\Omega_i=\lambda_i\OmegaΩi​=λi​Ω, where the weight λi∈[0,1]\lambda_i\in[0,1]λi​∈[0,1] is the likelihood that iii's appeal creates binding precedent (in the paper this reuses the symbol λi\lambda_iλi​; it plays the role of ψi\psi_iψi​). Developers ignore Ωi\Omega_iΩi​ when choosing aia_iai​, while the social continuation value is:

Wilit,soc=Piappeal(λi+Ωi)(8.2.4.15)W_i^{lit,soc}=P_i^{appeal}\left(\lambda_i+\Omega_i\right) \tag{8.2.4.15}Wilit,soc​=Piappeal​(λi​+Ωi​)(8.2.4.15)

Hence private appeal, and thus precedent formation, is below the social optimum.

Estimation

The parameters are recovered in three steps.

(1) Calibration, Θcalibrate=[ctlit,κcomp]\Theta^{calibrate}=[ct^{lit},\kappa^{comp}]Θcalibrate=[ctlit,κcomp]. State trial-court cases are not fully observed, so the lower-court litigation count ctlitct^{lit}ctlit (state-year) is imputed from state appellate cases using the federal appellate-to-trial ratio (0.25), and distributed over years with the federal lag distribution (weights 0.39, 0.39, 0.16, 0.06 for lags of 0–1, 1–2, 2–3, 3–4 years). The completion shock κ\kappaκ is the exogenous withdrawal rate by transmission owner-year in the interconnection queues (2010–2024).

(2) Probit outside the model, Θprobit=[ψ]\Theta^{probit}=[\psi]Θprobit=[ψ] (10 parameters): precedent formation conditional on appeal, using court-level and case-level legal characteristics. Since precedent formation is determined by courts and not by developers, it can be estimated separately.

(3) SMM for the remaining 23 parameters:

ΘSMM=[CE,α,θπ⏟Entry / Permit, CA,ζ,γ,ψ0⏟Appeal / Precedent, ρEL,ρLA⏟Selection Corr](8.2.4.19)\Theta^{SMM}=[\underbrace{C_E,\alpha,\theta_\pi}_{\text{Entry / Permit}},\ \underbrace{C_A,\zeta,\gamma,\psi_0}_{\text{Appeal / Precedent}},\ \underbrace{\rho_{EL},\rho_{LA}}_{\text{Selection Corr}}] \tag{8.2.4.19}ΘSMM=[Entry / PermitCE​,α,θπ​​​, Appeal / PrecedentCA​,ζ,γ,ψ0​​​, Selection CorrρEL​,ρLA​​​](8.2.4.19)

where α\alphaα has 12 elements and γ\gammaγ has 4.

Simulation: for each county-year, the number of potential entrants is the maximum number of actual entrants ever observed in that county. Continuation values of the appellate stage are computed first by backward induction, then entry is simulated forward with the logit. Among entrants in each state-year, exactly ctlitct^{lit}ctlit projects are assigned to litigation by an exponential race: each entrant gets a random clock that ticks faster when its logistic litigation propensity (driven by z2z_2z2​) is higher, shifted by a common constant so that the total matches ctlitct^{lit}ctlit; the first clocks to go off are litigated. Litigated projects then make appeal decisions, and appeal outcomes and precedent formation are simulated.

The estimator matches K=40K=40K=40 moments (mostly counts by bins):

where g(Θ)g(\Theta)g(Θ) is the vector of percentage deviations between simulated moments M(Θ)M(\Theta)M(Θ) and empirical moments MMM, and Ω^\hat{\Omega}Ω^ is the variance-covariance matrix of the empirical moments from 200 bootstrap replications.   • CEC_ECE​: overall level of entry.

  • α0,αL,αD,αC\alpha_0,\alpha_L,\alpha_D,\alpha_Cα0​,αL​,αD​,αC​: entry across bins of the legal environment, permit backlog and county characteristics.

  • θπ\theta_\piθπ​: response of entry to computed project NPV.

  • CAC_ACA​: overall appeal frequency among litigated projects, and its variation across lower-court caseload z2z_2z2​, appellate caseload z3z_3z3​ and legal environment bins.

  • γ0,γL\gamma_0,\gamma_Lγ0​,γL​: appeal win rates, overall and across legal environment bins.

  • ψ0\psi_0ψ0​: overall frequency with which appeals create precedent.

The correlations are identified by stage-specific excluded shifters:

  • z1z_1z1​: transmission line density in 1993, which proxies long-run siting fundamentals and grid access and shifts entry only.

  • z2z_2z2​: lower-court caseload (backlog), which shifts litigation through longer expected processing time and is excluded from entry payoffs and appeal decisions.

  • z3z_3z3​: appellate caseload, which shifts appeal conditional on litigation.

ρEL\rho_{EL}ρEL​ is identified from variation in entry across z2z_2z2​ bins conditional on z1z_1z1​: since z2z_2z2​ only shifts litigation, systematic differences in entry across z2z_2z2​ must come from correlated unobservables. ρLA\rho_{LA}ρLA​ is identified from appeal rates among litigated projects across z2z_2z2​ bins, which reflect the composition of the litigated pool. (ρEA\rho_{EA}ρEA​ would be identified from entry across z3z_3z3​ bins, but is normalized to 0.)

Counterfactuals

Baseline simulated entry is 16,395 projects.

  • Maximal precedent (comparative static): each county is given its maximum observed level of precedent from the start. Entry rises to 16,818 (+2.6%), the informational value of legal clarity alone.

  • Legal fee-shifting: prevailing parties recover attorney and expert fees (as in the Clean Air Act citizen-suit provision), modeled as a 5–40% reduction in CAC_ACA​. A 20% reduction raises entry to 17,388 (+6.1%).

  • Legal protection insurance: the same 20% reduction in CAC_ACA​, but developers pay an upfront premium proxied by 20/K20/K20/K percent of expected net profit (K=15K=15K=15). Entry rises by 1.6%.

  • Streamlined permitting: a 5–40% reduction in CpermC^{perm}Cperm. A 20% reduction raises entry to 16,946 (+3.4%).

Policies that directly lower the private cost of legal proceedings, and thus address the under-investment in precedent, raise entry more than administrative reforms that only lower permitting costs.
