Model Setting

Households make a dynamic decision on whether and when to adopt residential solar photovoltaic (PV) systems. In each period ttt, a household iii can either not adopt, j=0j=0j=0, or adopt one of JJJ PV systems with different capacity sizes, j=1,…,Jj=1,\ldots,Jj=1,…,J. Adoption is a terminating action: once a household installs a PV system, it exits the adoption problem, while non-adoption preserves the option to adopt in a future period.

Household iii chooses the alternative that maximizes:

Uijt=vijt+ϵijt,(8.1.1.1)U_{ijt}=v_{ijt}+\epsilon_{ijt}, \tag{8.1.1.1}Uijt​=vijt​+ϵijt​,(8.1.1.1)

where ϵijt\epsilon_{ijt}ϵijt​ follows an i.i.d. Type-I extreme value distribution. In the baseline aggregate model, households are homogeneous apart from these idiosyncratic taste shocks.

For an adoption alternative j=1,…,Jj=1,\ldots,Jj=1,…,J, the conditional value is:

Estimating Equation

Combining the dynamic value of waiting with the market share inversion gives the main estimating equation:

where:

ejt=ξjt−β(ξ1,t+1−ηt).(8.1.1.19)e_{jt} = \xi_{jt} - \beta \left( \xi_{1,t+1}-\eta_t \right). \tag{8.1.1.19}ejt​=ξjt​−β(ξ1,t+1​−ηt​).(8.1.1.19)

The equation relates current adoption to current and future investment opportunities. For forward-looking households, adoption today is less attractive when future PV prices are expected to fall, while a pre-announced reduction in future subsidies increases adoption before the policy change.

If β\betaβ were known and all explanatory variables were exogenous, the equation could be estimated linearly. In practice, β\betaβ is unknown and enters both directly and nonlinearly through pjt(β)p_{jt}(\beta)pjt​(β), so the model is estimated using nonlinear GMM.

Identification and GMM Estimation

The model is estimated using the moment conditions:

E[zjtejt]=0,(8.1.1.20)E[z_{jt}e_{jt}]=0, \tag{8.1.1.20}E[zjt​ejt​]=0,(8.1.1.20)

where zjtz_{jt}zjt​ is a vector of instruments.

The upfront investment price may be endogenous because installers can charge higher prices when demand is high. To identify the price sensitivity α\alphaα, the authors use the price index of Chinese PV modules in the European market as a cost shifter. Module prices are strongly related to installation costs but are assumed not to respond directly to local PV demand shocks.

The discount factor β\betaβ is identified primarily from variation in the future GCC benefits. The GCC subsidy schedule varies substantially over time and across capacity choices, while the upfront investment cost enters current utility separately. This variation identifies how households trade off current investment costs against future financial benefits.

A second source of identification comes from pre-announced changes in GCC subsidies. A future reduction in subsidies does not affect the payoff from adopting before the reform, but lowers the value of waiting and adopting afterward. The resulting increase in adoption immediately before subsidy reductions therefore contains information about the discounting of future adoption opportunities.

Local Market Heterogeneity

The baseline model is extended to allow for persistent household heterogeneity across MMM local markets. For households in market mmm, the conditional value of adopting alternative jjj becomes:

vmjt=xjtγ−αpjt+ξjt+wjtλm,(8.1.1.25)v_{mjt} = x_{jt}\gamma - \alpha p_{jt} + \xi_{jt} + w_{jt}\lambda_m, \tag{8.1.1.25}vmjt​=xjt​γ−αpjt​+ξjt​+wjt​λm​,(8.1.1.25)

where:

λm=ΛDm,(8.1.1.26)\lambda_m=\Lambda D_m, \tag{8.1.1.26}λm​=ΛDm​,(8.1.1.26)

and DmD_mDm​ contains local demographic characteristics such as income, household size, house size, and local-market fixed effects. The interactions allow price sensitivity and preferences for PV capacity to vary systematically across markets.

Because adoption shares are frequently zero at the highly disaggregated local-market level, the authors do not directly apply the Berry share inversion within each market. Instead, they combine the aggregate GMM moments with micro-moments derived from the local-market likelihood. These moments match demographic-product correlations and total local adoption rates between the data and the model. The next-period local choice probability entering the dynamic model is estimated in a first stage.
