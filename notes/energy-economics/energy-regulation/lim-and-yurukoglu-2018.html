Model Setting

The model is an infinite-horizon dynamic game between an electricity distribution utility and a regulator. Each period corresponds to one year. The payoff-relevant state variables are the utility's existing capital stock, ktk_tkt​, and the regulator's weight on consumer surplus relative to utility profits, αt\alpha_tαt​:

st=(kt,αt).(8.3.1.1)s_t=(k_t,\alpha_t). \tag{8.3.1.1}st​=(kt​,αt​).(8.3.1.1)

In each period, the regulator first chooses the regulated rate of return, rtr_trt​, and the leniency of auditing, κt∈[0,1]\kappa_t\in[0,1]κt​∈[0,1], where 1−κt1-\kappa_t1−κt​ measures audit intensity. After observing (rt,κt)(r_t,\kappa_t)(rt​,κt​), the utility chooses next period's capital stock, kt+1k_{t+1}kt+1​, and managerial effort, ete_tet​. The regulator preference parameter αt\alpha_tαt​ evolves exogenously according to a first-order Markov process, while the capital stock evolves endogenously through investment.

Consumers have inelastic electricity demand up to a choke price that increases with the utility's capital stock:

D(p)={Q,p≤pˉ+β~log⁡(k/N),0,otherwise.(8.3.1.2)D(p)= \begin{cases} Q, & p\leq \bar{p}+\tilde{\beta}\log(k/N),\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \tag{8.3.1.2}D(p)={Q,0,​p≤pˉ​+β~​log(k/N),otherwise.​(8.3.1.2)

A larger capital stock improves reliability and therefore increases consumers' willingness to pay.

The regulated price satisfies the utility's revenue requirement:

pQ=rk+pfQ[1+κ(eˉ−e+ε)],(8.3.1.3)pQ=rk+p^fQ\left[1+\kappa(\bar{e}-e+\varepsilon)\right], \tag{8.3.1.3}pQ=rk+pfQ[1+κ(eˉ−e+ε)],(8.3.1.3)

where pfp^fpf is the wholesale electricity price, eˉ−e+ε\bar{e}-e+\varepsiloneˉ−e+ε determines electricity loss, and κ\kappaκ is the fraction of loss-related input costs passed through to consumers. A higher κ\kappaκ therefore corresponds to weaker auditing.

The Utility's Problem

The utility chooses investment and managerial effort. Define investment as:

I=k′−(1−δ)k,(8.3.1.4)I=k'-(1-\delta)k, \tag{8.3.1.4}I=k′−(1−δ)k,(8.3.1.4)

where δ\deltaδ is the depreciation rate. After substituting the regulated price into profits, the utility's per-period payoff can be written as:

π=rk−I−hI2+(κ−1)pfQ(eˉ−e+ε)−γee2+σIu,(8.3.1.5)\pi = rk-I-hI^2 +(\kappa-1)p^fQ(\bar{e}-e+\varepsilon) -\gamma_e e^2 +\sigma_I u, \tag{8.3.1.5}π=rk−I−hI2+(κ−1)pfQ(eˉ−e+ε)−γe​e2+σI​u,(8.3.1.5)

where hhh is the quadratic investment adjustment cost, γe\gamma_eγe​ is the cost of managerial effort, and uuu is an investment-specific extreme-value shock.

The utility solves:

Vu(k,α)=max⁡k′,e{E[π(k′,e;k,r,κ)]+βE[Vu(k′,α′)]}.(8.3.1.6)V^u(k,\alpha) = \max_{k',e} \left\{ E[\pi(k',e;k,r,\kappa)] + \beta E[V^u(k',\alpha')] \right\}. \tag{8.3.1.6}Vu(k,α)=k′,emax​{E[π(k′,e;k,r,κ)]+βE[Vu(k′,α′)]}.(8.3.1.6)

e∗(κ)=min⁡{(1−κ)pfQ2γe,eˉ}.(8.3.1.7)e^*(\kappa) = \min \left\{ \frac{(1-\kappa)p^fQ}{2\gamma_e}, \bar{e} \right\}. \tag{8.3.1.7}e∗(κ)=min{2γe​(1−κ)pfQ​,eˉ}.(8.3.1.7)

κ↑⇒e∗↓⇒energy loss↑.(8.3.1.8)\kappa\uparrow \quad\Rightarrow\quad e^*\downarrow \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{energy loss}\uparrow. \tag{8.3.1.8}κ↑⇒e∗↓⇒energy loss↑.(8.3.1.8)

Investment is dynamic because the return to newly installed capital depends on future regulated rates of return. In particular, the current return rtr_trt​ applies to the existing capital stock, while current investment is determined by expectations over future policies:

kt+1=f(kt,αt,Et[rt+1,rt+2,...]).(8.3.1.9)k_{t+1} = f\left(k_t,\alpha_t, E_t[r_{t+1},r_{t+2},...]\right). \tag{8.3.1.9}kt+1​=f(kt​,αt​,Et​[rt+1​,rt+2​,...]).(8.3.1.9)

The Regulator's Problem

The regulator chooses the rate of return and audit policy to balance consumer welfare and utility value. Its objective is:

UR=E[CV]αE[Vu]1−α−γκ(1−κ)2−γr(r−rm)2,(8.3.1.10)U^R = E[CV]^{\alpha} E[V^u]^{1-\alpha} - \gamma_{\kappa}(1-\kappa)^2 - \gamma_r(r-r^m)^2, \tag{8.3.1.10}UR=E[CV]αE[Vu]1−α−γκ​(1−κ)2−γr​(r−rm)2,(8.3.1.10)

where CVCVCV is the discounted value of consumer surplus, rmr^mrm is the benchmark market rate of return, γκ\gamma_{\kappa}γκ​ is the cost of auditing, and γr\gamma_rγr​ is the cost of deviating from the benchmark return.

The auditing cost captures the institutional cost of challenging the automatic pass-through of electricity input costs. The return-adjustment cost captures constraints that prevent regulators from setting rates of return far from prevailing market returns.

The regulator's preference for consumer surplus is parameterized as:

αit=a0+a1Repit+a2Nominateit,(8.3.1.11)\alpha_{it} = a_0+a_1 Rep_{it}+a_2 Nominate_{it}, \tag{8.3.1.11}αit​=a0​+a1​Repit​+a2​Nominateit​,(8.3.1.11)

where RepitRep_{it}Repit​ is the fraction of Republican commissioners on the state public utility commission and NominateitNominate_{it}Nominateit​ measures the political ideology of the state.

The Equilibrium

The equilibrium concept is Markov Perfect Equilibrium. The regulator follows policy functions:

r=r(k,α),κ=κ(k,α),(8.3.1.12)r=r(k,\alpha), \qquad \kappa=\kappa(k,\alpha), \tag{8.3.1.12}r=r(k,α),κ=κ(k,α),(8.3.1.12)

and the utility follows:

k′=k′(k,α,r,κ,u),e=e(k,α,r,κ,u).(8.3.1.13)k'=k'(k,\alpha,r,\kappa,u), \qquad e=e(k,\alpha,r,\kappa,u). \tag{8.3.1.13}k′=k′(k,α,r,κ,u),e=e(k,α,r,κ,u).(8.3.1.13)

An equilibrium requires that the utility's policies are optimal given the regulator's policies, the regulator's policies are optimal given the utility's behavior, and the value functions are consistent with the discounted stream of payoffs generated by these policies.

Because future regulators re-optimize as a function of the realized state, the regulator cannot commit to a future path of rates of return. This creates a time inconsistency problem:

Lack of commitment→Et[rt+1,rt+2,...]↓→Investmentt↓.(8.3.1.14)\text{Lack of commitment} \rightarrow E_t[r_{t+1},r_{t+2},...]\downarrow \rightarrow Investment_t\downarrow. \tag{8.3.1.14}Lack of commitment→Et​[rt+1​,rt+2​,...]↓→Investmentt​↓.(8.3.1.14)

Model Estimation

The authors estimate eight structural parameters:

θ=(γe,γκ,h,γr,σI,a0,a1,a2).(8.3.1.15)\theta= (\gamma_e,\gamma_{\kappa},h,\gamma_r, \sigma_I,a_0,a_1,a_2). \tag{8.3.1.15}θ=(γe​,γκ​,h,γr​,σI​,a0​,a1​,a2​).(8.3.1.15)

The remaining parameters are calibrated to represent a typical utility. In particular, the annual discount factor is set to β=0.96\beta=0.96β=0.96, the depreciation rate to δ=0.041\delta=0.041δ=0.041, the wholesale electricity price to pf=$70p^f=\$70pf=$70 per MWh, and the benchmark return to rm=10%r^m=10\%rm=10%. The representative utility serves 500,000 customers and delivers 15 million MWh per year.

The estimation uses a two-step procedure following Bajari, Benkard and Levin (2007) and Pakes, Ostrovsky and Berry (2007). The main idea is to first estimate equilibrium policy functions directly from the data and then choose structural parameters such that these observed policies are approximately optimal under the model.

For every candidate parameter vector θ\thetaθ, the estimation proceeds as follows.

First, the political variables are mapped into the latent regulator preference:

α^it=a0+a1Repit+a2Nominateit.(8.3.1.16)\hat{\alpha}_{it} = a_0+a_1 Rep_{it}+a_2 Nominate_{it}. \tag{8.3.1.16}α^it​=a0​+a1​Repit​+a2​Nominateit​.(8.3.1.16)

The state space is discretized into 20 capital states and 9 regulator-preference states. The implied series of αit\alpha_{it}αit​ is then used to estimate a first-order Markov transition process:

Pr(αt+1∣αt).(8.3.1.17)Pr(\alpha_{t+1}|\alpha_t). \tag{8.3.1.17}Pr(αt+1​∣αt​).(8.3.1.17)

Second, managerial effort is recovered from observed electricity loss. Let litl_{it}lit​ denote electricity loss. The model implies:

lit=Qit(eˉ−eit+εit).(8.3.1.18)l_{it} = Q_{it}(\bar{e}-e_{it}+\varepsilon_{it}). \tag{8.3.1.18}lit​=Qit​(eˉ−eit​+εit​).(8.3.1.18)

Assuming E[εit]=0E[\varepsilon_{it}]=0E[εit​]=0, effort is estimated by:

e^it=eˉ−litQit.(8.3.1.19)\hat{e}_{it} = \bar{e}-\frac{l_{it}}{Q_{it}}. \tag{8.3.1.19}e^it​=eˉ−Qit​lit​​.(8.3.1.19)

Third, the unobserved auditing policy is recovered using the utility's first-order condition for effort:

κ^it=1−2γee^itQpf.(8.3.1.20)\hat{\kappa}_{it} = 1-\frac{2\gamma_e\hat{e}_{it}}{Qp^f}. \tag{8.3.1.20}κ^it​=1−Qpf2γe​e^it​​.(8.3.1.20)

Fourth, the authors estimate policy functions for investment, effort, the regulated rate of return, and auditing as linear functions of the state variables:

policyit=f(kit,αit),(8.3.1.21)policy_{it} = f(k_{it},\alpha_{it}), \tag{8.3.1.21}policyit​=f(kit​,αit​),(8.3.1.21)

where the policy variables are investment, e^it\hat e_{it}e^it​, ritr_{it}rit​, and κ^it\hat\kappa_{it}κ^it​.

Fifth, starting from every point in the state-space grid, the authors forward simulate 300 paths of 200 periods using the estimated policy functions and the Markov process for α\alphaα. The discounted streams of utility profits and consumer surplus are used to construct:

V^u(k,α)andCV^(k,α).(8.3.1.22)\hat V^u(k,\alpha) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \widehat{CV}(k,\alpha). \tag{8.3.1.22}V^u(k,α)andCV(k,α).(8.3.1.22)

Sixth, conditional on the candidate parameters and simulated value functions, the optimal policy of each player is calculated while holding the opponent's estimated policy fixed. This produces model-implied optimal policies:

I∗(k,α;θ),e∗(k,α;θ),r∗(k,α;θ),κ∗(k,α;θ).(8.3.1.23)I^*(k,\alpha;\theta),\quad e^*(k,\alpha;\theta),\quad r^*(k,\alpha;\theta),\quad \kappa^*(k,\alpha;\theta). \tag{8.3.1.23}I∗(k,α;θ),e∗(k,α;θ),r∗(k,α;θ),κ∗(k,α;θ).(8.3.1.23)

The structural parameters are chosen so that the policy functions observed in the data are close to the model-implied optimal policies and so that the model reproduces a set of empirical moments. Let G(θ)G(\theta)G(θ) denote the vector of moment conditions. It contains four average policy-function discrepancies, the means and variances of the rate of return, investment, and energy loss, the mean auditing leniency, and two regression coefficients relating political variables to effort and regulated returns.

The estimator is:

θ^=arg⁡min⁡θG(θ)′WG(θ),(8.3.1.24)\hat{\theta} = \arg\min_{\theta} G(\theta)'WG(\theta), \tag{8.3.1.24}θ^=argθmin​G(θ)′WG(θ),(8.3.1.24)

where WWW is a weighting matrix that adjusts for differences in scale across moments. Confidence intervals are constructed using a block bootstrap clustered at the utility-state level.

Identification

The structural parameters are identified by different features of the data. The effort cost parameter, γe\gamma_eγe​, is primarily disciplined by mean managerial effort, while the investment adjustment cost, hhh, is closely related to mean investment. The scale of the investment-specific shock, σI\sigma_IσI​, is identified mainly from the dispersion of investment, and the return-adjustment cost, γr\gamma_rγr​, is closely related to the average regulated rate of return.

The political preference parameters, a1a_1a1​ and a2a_2a2​, are identified by the relationships between political variables and the regulator's policies. In the model, a lower α\alphaα implies a higher regulated return and weaker auditing. The estimation therefore uses the empirical relationships between political variables and both regulated returns and managerial effort to recover how political environments map into regulator preferences.
