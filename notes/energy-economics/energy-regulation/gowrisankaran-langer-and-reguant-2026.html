A Simplified Model

The regulator sets electricity rates, rrr, on electricity demand, or load, lll, which we approximate as being perfectly inelastic. The regulator requires the utility to meet load and chooses rrr such that revenues equal the sum of variable costs, TVC, and an allowable return, sss, on the rate base, BBB. Utility revenues are then:

r×l=TVC+s×B(8.3.2.1)r\times l=TVC+s\times B \tag{8.3.2.1}r×l=TVC+s×B(8.3.2.1)

Consider an environment where the utility maximizes profits when facing a fixed load and one-generation technology, coal, that lasts two periods. We represent the per-unit cost of investment with δCOAL\delta^{COAL}δCOAL, the amount of capacity with KCOALK^{COAL}KCOAL, and the marginal cost with MCCOALMC^{COAL}MCCOAL. π≡s×αCOAL×KCOAL−δCOAL×KCOAL(8.3.2.2)\pi\equiv s\times \alpha^{COAL}\times K^{COAL}-\delta^{COAL}\times K^{COAL} \tag{8.3.2.2}π≡s×αCOAL×KCOAL−δCOAL×KCOAL(8.3.2.2)

We model incentive regulation by allowing the regulator to offer a RoR that is a declining function of reported costs. To manage informational asymmetries while avoiding ex post renegotiation, the regulator allows capacity that is "used and useful" to contribute more to the rate base. This combines the principle that only "prudent" capital investments should be included in the rate base with the fact that capacity's usage is one objective way to measure its prudence. We thus extend our simple model to add a used-and-useful standard. We model capital's contribution to the rate base with a simple logit functional form:

This is the revenue requirement of rate-of-return (RoR) regulation, and it is an accounting rule imposed by the regulator rather than an equilibrium outcome. The left side, r×lr\times lr×l, is what ratepayers pay: the rate rrr (dollars per MWh) times load lll (MWh), where load is treated as perfectly inelastic so that it does not respond to rrr. The right side is what the regulator lets the utility collect: total variable costs TVCTVCTVC (fuel and other operating costs), passed through one for one, plus an allowed return sss on the rate base BBB, the regulatory value of the utility's capital.

Because lll is fixed, the rule pins down the rate once costs and capital are known:

r=TVC+s×Bl.r=\frac{TVC+s\times B}{l}.r=lTVC+s×B​.

Two features matter for what follows. First, variable costs earn no margin, so the utility is indifferent about fuel cost savings. Second, the only source of profit is s×Bs\times Bs×B, so the utility cares about how much capital is counted in BBB and at what return. With inelastic load there is no demand-side discipline on rrr, so any discipline must come from how the regulator sets sss and BBB: equation (3) lets sss fall with rrr (incentive regulation), and equations (4) and (5) let BBB depend on utilization (the used-and-useful standard). Equation (2) uses this rule to compute profits.

Profit is revenue minus variable cost minus the cost of investment. The utility owns only coal capacity KCOALK^{COAL}KCOAL, bought at a per-unit cost δCOAL\delta^{COAL}δCOAL, so

π=r×l−TVC−δCOAL×KCOAL.\pi = r\times l - TVC - \delta^{COAL}\times K^{COAL}.π=r×l−TVC−δCOAL×KCOAL.

Equation (1) says that revenue net of variable cost equals the allowed return on the rate base, r×l−TVC=s×Br\times l-TVC=s\times Br×l−TVC=s×B. Substituting removes the rate, load and variable cost from profits:

π=s×B−δCOAL×KCOAL.\pi = s\times B - \delta^{COAL}\times K^{COAL}.π=s×B−δCOAL×KCOAL.
