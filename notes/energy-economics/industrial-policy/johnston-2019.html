Model Setting

The model studies a wind developer's choice between two subsidy schemes: the Production Tax Credit (PTC) and the Section 1603 cash grant. Project characteristics are taken as given when the subsidy choice is made. Let CiC_iCi​ denote the installation cost of project iii and MiM_iMi​ denote its expected annual electricity generation.

The key structural parameter is p∈[0,1]p\in[0,1]p∈[0,1], which measures the developer's valuation of one dollar of nonrefundable tax benefits relative to one dollar of cash. A value of p=1p=1p=1 implies that tax credits and cash are valued equally, while p<1p<1p<1 captures the loss in value associated with the nonrefundability of tax credits.

If project iii chooses the PTC, it receives a per-MWh tax credit for the first ten years of operation. The expected present value of the PTC is

ViPTC=∑t=110βtpE[MWhit]PTC(8.5.1.1)V_i^{PTC} = \sum_{t=1}^{10} \beta^t p E[MWh_{it}]PTC \tag{8.5.1.1}ViPTC​=t=1∑10​βtpE[MWhit​]PTC(8.5.1.1)

where β\betaβ is the real discount factor. Assuming expected annual generation is constant over time, E[MWhit]=MiE[MWh_{it}]=M_iE[MWhit​]=Mi​, this can be written as

ViPTC=pk2iMi(8.5.1.2)V_i^{PTC} = pk_{2i}M_i \tag{8.5.1.2}ViPTC​=pk2i​Mi​(8.5.1.2)

where k2ik_{2i}k2i​ is determined by the discount factor and the PTC schedule.

If the project chooses the grant, it receives 30% of installation cost. However, choosing the grant reduces the depreciable tax basis by 15% of installation cost. The present value of the grant is therefore

ViGrant=0.3Ci−p⋅0.15k1iCi(8.5.1.3)V_i^{Grant} = 0.3C_i - p\cdot 0.15 k_{1i}C_i \tag{8.5.1.3}ViGrant​=0.3Ci​−p⋅0.15k1i​Ci​(8.5.1.3)

where k1ik_{1i}k1i​ captures the present value of depreciation deductions, given the tax rate and discount factor.

The developer chooses the subsidy with the higher expected value:

max⁡{pk2iMi,  0.3Ci−p⋅0.15k1iCi}.(8.5.1.4)\max \left\{ pk_{2i}M_i,\; 0.3C_i-p\cdot0.15k_{1i}C_i \right\}. \tag{8.5.1.4}max{pk2i​Mi​,0.3Ci​−p⋅0.15k1i​Ci​}.(8.5.1.4)

Thus, more productive projects are more likely to choose the PTC, while higher-cost projects are more likely to choose the grant.

Let GiG_iGi​ be an indicator equal to one if project iii chooses the grant. To allow for unobserved determinants of subsidy choice, the model introduces an additive error term. Since the variance of these unobservables is assumed to increase with project size, the subsidy values are normalized by nameplate capacity. Let

Maximum Likelihood Estimation

The cost equation and the subsidy-choice equation are jointly estimated by maximum likelihood. For projects that choose the grant, both the subsidy choice and installation cost are observed. Their likelihood contribution can be written schematically as

Li(Gi=1)=f(Ci′∣Xi)×Pr(Gi=1∣Ci′,Mi′,Xi).(8.5.1.9)L_i(G_i=1) = f(C_i'|X_i) \times Pr(G_i=1|C_i',M_i',X_i). \tag{8.5.1.9}Li​(Gi​=1)=f(Ci′​∣Xi​)×Pr(Gi​=1∣Ci′​,Mi′​,Xi​).(8.5.1.9)

For projects that choose the PTC, installation cost is unobserved. The likelihood therefore integrates over all possible values of the latent installation cost:

Li(Gi=0)=∫Pr(Gi=0∣Ci′,Mi′,Xi)f(Ci′∣Xi)dCi′.(8.5.1.10)L_i(G_i=0) = \int Pr(G_i=0|C_i',M_i',X_i) f(C_i'|X_i) dC_i'. \tag{8.5.1.10}Li​(Gi​=0)=∫Pr(Gi​=0∣Ci′​,Mi′​,Xi​)f(Ci′​∣Xi​)dCi′​.(8.5.1.10)

Under the joint normality assumption, this integral can be expressed using the normal CDF. The parameters {p,γ,σu,σe,λ}\{p,\gamma,\sigma_u,\sigma_e,\lambda\}{p,γ,σu​,σe​,λ} are estimated jointly by maximizing the likelihood over all projects.

Identification

The valuation parameter ppp is identified from how projects trade off installation cost against expected electricity generation when choosing between the two subsidies. Higher Mi′M_i'Mi′​ increases the value of the PTC, whereas higher Ci′C_i'Ci′​ increases the value of the grant. The observed switching pattern between the two subsidies therefore identifies the relative valuation of tax credits and cash.

To identify the selection model, expected generation per unit of capacity, Mi′M_i'Mi′​, is excluded from the cost equation. It directly affects the subsidy choice through the value of the PTC but is assumed not to directly affect construction cost conditional on XiX_iXi​:

(Mi′,Xi)⊥(ei,ui).(8.5.1.11)(M_i',X_i)\perp(e_i,u_i). \tag{8.5.1.11}(Mi′​,Xi​)⊥(ei​,ui​).(8.5.1.11)

Thus, Mi′M_i'Mi′​ shifts the probability of selecting the grant without directly shifting the conditional mean of installation cost.

The valuation parameter ppp is difficult to separately identify from the discount factor β\betaβ. The paper therefore calibrates β\betaβ using a 7% real discount rate rather than jointly estimating it with ppp.

Main Estimate

Under the baseline assumption of a 7% real discount rate, the estimated valuation parameter is p^=0.85\hat{p}=0.85p^​=0.85. The estimate implies that wind developers value one dollar of nonrefundable tax credits at approximately $0.85 relative to one dollar of cash grant.
