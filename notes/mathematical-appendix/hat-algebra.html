Hat Algebra

Hat Algebta is the Jones (1965) technique for comparative static analysis in trade models. Totally differentiating a model in logarithms of variables yields a linear system relating small proportional changes (denoted by carats (x^\hat{x}x^), or "hats") via elasticities and shares. As published it used x∗x^*x∗, not x^\hat{x}x^, due to typographical constraints. This name was used first in print by Brecher and Feenstra (1983).

In mathematics, the hat algebra is:

x^=dln(x)=dxx(10.1.0.1)\hat{x}=dln(x)=\frac{dx}{x} \tag{10.1.0.1}x^=dln(x)=xdx​(10.1.0.1)

Exact Hat Algebra

Dekle, Eaton and Kortum (2008) used the same notation x^\hat{x}x^ for comparison of two equilibria that need not be close enough to be captured by differential calculus. Letting xxx and x′x^{\prime}x′ be values of a variable in two equilibria, then x^=x′x\hat{x}=\frac{x^{\prime}}{x}x^=xx′​. Costinot and Rodríguez-Clare (2014) (Online Appendix) called this the exact hat algebra.

In mathematics, the hat algebra is:

x^=x′x(10.1.0.2)\hat{x}=\frac{x^{\prime}}{x} \tag{10.1.0.2}x^=xx′​(10.1.0.2)
