Elasticity of Substitution

The elasticity of substitution is proposed by Hicks (1946) and defined as:

1σ=dln[FLFK]dlnKL(10.8.0.3)\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{d\text{ln}[\frac{F_L}{F_K}]}{d\text{ln}\frac{K}{L}} \tag{10.8.0.3}σ1​=dlnLK​dln[FK​FL​​]​(10.8.0.3)

In this expression, the elasticity of substitution is defined as the relative change in the marginal rate of technical substitution (MRTS) when the input ratio KL\frac{K}{L}LK​ changes. When KL\frac{K}{L}LK​ increases, FLFK\frac{F_L}{F_K}FK​FL​​ increases, so σ\sigmaσ is always positive. If FLFK\frac{F_L}{F_K}FK​FL​​ changes rapidly with KL\frac{K}{L}LK​, it indicates that substitution is difficult, and σ\sigmaσ is small. If FLFK\frac{F_L}{F_K}FK​FL​​ changes slowly with KL\frac{K}{L}LK​, it indicates that substitution is easy, and σ\sigmaσ is large.

In a competitive market, firms are price takers, which means w=FLw=F_Lw=FL​ and r=FKr=F_Kr=FK​. Therefore, we have a new version of the elasticity of substitution:

σ=dln[KL]dln[wr](10.8.0.4)\sigma=\frac{d\text{ln}[\frac{K}{L}]}{d\text{ln}[\frac{w}{r}]} \tag{10.8.0.4}σ=dln[rw​]dln[LK​]​(10.8.0.4)

The basic intuition is: when the relative price of labor to capital (w/rw/rw/r) increases, labor becomes more expensive, leading firms to substitute labor with capital. As a result, the capital-to-labor ratio (K/LK/LK/L) rises. Therefore, the elasticity of substitution σ\sigmaσ reflects the sensitivity of the capital-labor ratio to changes in the relative price of labor to capital. If σ\sigmaσ is large, it indicates that firms are highly responsive to price changes and can easily substitute capital for labor or vice versa. Conversely, if σ\sigmaσ is small, it indicates that firms are less responsive to price changes, making it difficult to substitute between inputs.
