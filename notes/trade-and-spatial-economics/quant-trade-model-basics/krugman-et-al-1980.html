Reference: Krugman et al. (1980); Allen and Arkolakis Notes

Model Features: (1) Increasing returns to scale (fixed entry cost); (2) Preference on consumption diversity (higher diversity leads to higher welfare); (3) Each country can produce many types of goods, and the number of goods is endogenously determined; (4) All firms are homogeneous (Melitz (2003) will introduce heterogeneous firm model).

Model Setup

The basic model setup in the model is same as Armington Model (See Armington (1969) for details). The main difference is, in this model, suppose that there is a continuum Ω\OmegaΩ of possible varieties that the world can produce, and suppose that every firm in the world produces a distinct variety ω∈Ω\omega\in \Omegaω∈Ω. Let the set of varieties produced by firms located in country i∈Si\in Si∈S be denoted by Ωi⊂Ω\Omega_i\subset \OmegaΩi​⊂Ω.

A key feature of the model is that there are increasing returns to scale. To succinctly model the increasing returns from scale, we suppose that a firm has to incur a fixed entry cost fief_i^efie​ in order to produce. Let the productivity of all firms producing in country iii be homogeneous and denoted by AiA_iAi​, i.e. it costs 1Ai\frac{1}{A_i}Ai​1​ units of labor to produce one unit of the differentiated variety. Finally, we suppose that firms are subject to iceberg trade costs {τij}i,j∈S\{\tau_{ij}\}_{i,j\in S}{τij​}i,j∈S​.
